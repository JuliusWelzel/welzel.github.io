Preprocessing and reporting practices in mobile EEG, and the development of a reporting standard for mobile EEG studies.

Programme META-REP, DFG Priority Programme SPP 2317

Focus Reporting standard for mobile EEG studies

The programme

META-REP (A Meta-scientific Programme to Analyse and Optimise Replicability in the Behavioural, Social, and Cognitive Sciences) is a DFG Priority Programme (SPP 2317) on the replicability of findings and on methods to improve it. In Oldenburg, the METEOR project addresses the many defensible analysis decisions between a raw neural recording and a published result. My work within META-REP concerns these decisions in mobile EEG.

Background

Mobile EEG allows recordings during walking and other everyday movements. Compared with stationary EEG, it involves additional methodological decisions: hardware and cable management, synchronisation with motion sensors, detection of gait events, and the removal of movement artefacts, including the order and parameters of preprocessing steps.

In a review of studies combining mobile EEG with gait measurements (Vinod et al., 2026), we found large differences in the selection, order and combination of preprocessing steps, in particular in the tools and reporting of artefact rejection. Many pipelines were not described in enough detail to be reproduced. We concluded that standardised reporting, rather than a single mandatory pipeline, is needed to make studies comparable and reproducible. An earlier review of P3 studies during walking reached a similar conclusion (Jacobsen et al., 2025).

A reporting standard for mobile EEG

A reporting standard for mobile EEG studies is in preparation. It defines a minimum set of information to report, starting from the items most often missing in the review:

  • Recording context Setting and task, such as indoor or outdoor, treadmill or overground walking, and the equipment used.
  • Preprocessing steps Every step with its order and tools, in particular how movement artefacts are rejected.
  • Software parameters The settings of each tool, so that a pipeline can be re-run.
  • Open code and workflows Analysis code and workflows shared openly, ideally with data in community standards such as EEG-BIDS and Motion-BIDS.

Related work

  • EEGManySteps: multi-lab study of the influence of recording setups and pipelines on gait-related EEG.
  • IGOR umbrella review: umbrella review of how EEG/ERP preprocessing is reported and justified.
  • Multiverse analyses: mapping preprocessing choices in mobile EEG and a multiverse of beta activity during gait by Vaishali Vinod. The first maps preprocessing choices in the literature (Oldenburg METEOR team); the second varies preprocessing choices across two walking datasets.
